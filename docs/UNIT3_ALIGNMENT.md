# World History Unit 3 and the game

How the game lines up with the class's Unit 3, "The Printing Press" (lessons 3.1 to 3.4, the study guide and the unit test), and with the New York grade 9 inquiry it follows. The essential question for the unit and for the game's last chapter is the same:

> Did the printing press preserve the past or invent the future?

## Chapter 8: Past or Future? (grades 9–12)

Chapter 8 was written from the unit's materials. It is set in Aldus Manutius's print shop in Venice around 1500, and every line is at about a 9th-grade reading level, in English and Spanish. The reading-level setting doesn't change it. It appears only when the grade band is **9–12**.

| Unit lesson | Supporting question | Where it is in Chapter 8 |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | What was first printed? | The chained library: the Bible, Donatus's Latin grammar and indulgences, and why printers chose them |
| 3.2 | In what ways did the printing press preserve the past? | The chained library: scribes, chained books, the village priest, humanism and Greek texts |
| 3.3 | How did the printing press stimulate interest in exploration? | The press (print Columbus's 1493 letter) and the map table (the six cities where it was printed in 1493–94) |
| 3.4 | To what extent did the printing press facilitate change? | The counting house: merchant arithmetic, Dittmar's city-growth finding and the debate about it, and correcting maps in new editions |
| Essential question | Did it preserve the past or invent the future? | The journal check: a claim, two pieces of evidence and written reasoning, then the 3.2 paragraph prompt with the class's sentence starters |

The journal check follows the unit test: Gutenberg's innovation, cost of books, how people learned before printing, Marco Polo and Mandeville, the Columbus letter, Luther, and the two short-answer questions (Q28 and Q29).

Note on Chapter 8 for teachers: on a device where the teacher panel was set up before Chapter 8 existed, turn it on under **Chapters**, or add `8` to the group link's `chapters=` list.

## Things in the unit materials worth a second look

These came up while reading the materials. The game uses the corrected version where it matters.

- **Test Q6 answer key.** The key marks Latin. The class reading (3.1b, Debus) says the search for classical texts brought "a new awareness of the importance of Greek." The game uses Greek.
- **Test Q10** (the map question) has no answer marked in the key. B, "rapidly along major trade routes," is clearly intended.
- **Test Q19** repeats the answer choices from Q13 and has no answer marked.
- **Test Section 4** is labeled 2 points per question, but the instructions describe 2 points for a complete sentence plus 2 for correct information.
- **"Europe's first printed book—the Gutenberg Bible" (3.1 slides).** 3.1a says a Latin grammar and indulgences may be older, so the game calls the Bible Gutenberg's "first major book," as the test's Q3 does.
- **Columbus letter edition counts differ between sources:** Edney (3.3c) counts eleven editions in 1493, and Taylor (3.3a) nine. The game says "about a dozen."
- **"Announced the existence of the American continent" (3.3b).** Columbus believed he had reached islands near Asia. The game says so.
- **Dittmar's 60% finding (3.4c)** is a correlation. The reading notes that other historians think printing's economic effect was small, and the game presents it as a debate.
- **"Scribal veneration" gloss in 3.4a.** The handout explains it as "worship of ancestors"; it means scribes' reverence for ancient learning.
- **Spanish handouts** have a few machine-translation slips: "orfebre" for metalworker, "Arte menor" for Ars minor, and "El cuento del indulgente" for The Pardoner's Tale. The usual Spanish title is "El cuento del bulero."
